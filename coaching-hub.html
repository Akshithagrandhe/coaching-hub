Design strategy

This panel documents the reasoning behind CoachHub's UI decisions — treat it as a design walkthrough rather than a marketing page.

The core problem: trust, not features

Coaching marketplaces fail on trust before they fail on matching. A user who fears losing money to a fake coach won't browse profiles — they'll leave. Every screen here is built to answer "is my money safe?" before it answers "is this coach good?"

Layered trust signals

Trust cues appear at four altitudes: the notification bar (platform-wide promise), the hero metrics (proof, not claims), coach card badges (per-coach evidence), and the escrow modal (transaction-level guarantee). Repetition at decreasing scope mirrors how users actually verify claims — broad promise first, specific proof at the moment of decision.

Why the escrow flow is a visible tracker

Escrow is an abstract financial concept most users have never used outside eBay or freelance platforms. Rendering it as a three-node horizontal tracker — the same shape as a shipping tracker — borrows a mental model people already trust, instead of asking them to trust new legal language.

Why simulate the scam, not just warn about it

Static warning copy ("beware of off-platform payment") is routinely ignored — banner blindness applies to safety messaging too. Letting a user trigger the exact scenario and watch the platform intercept it builds procedural memory: they now recognize the pattern in the wild, not just the policy in a footer link.

Color psychology

Emerald is reserved for verified/safe states so it stays meaningful rather than decorative. Deep navy carries authority and institutional weight (used for the header, escrow strip, and floating CTA) — closer to a bank's palette than a typical coaching app's. Red is used exclusively for the scam simulation, so its appearance anywhere else would itself be a signal something is wrong.

Edge cases handled

  • Empty filter state (no matches) has a dedicated message instead of a blank grid.
  • Unverified coaches are never hidden by default — the toggle is opt-in, so users aren't misled into thinking the whole marketplace is pre-filtered.
  • Modal and drawer both trap focus visually, close on overlay click and Escape, and restore scroll on close.
  • Reduced-motion preference disables non-essential animation.

Accessibility notes

All interactive controls have visible focus rings, icon-only buttons carry aria-labels, the scam modal uses alertdialog semantics, and color is never the sole carrier of meaning — badges pair icon + text.

Scope note: this is a static front-end prototype. Booking, escrow, and reporting actions are simulated client-side to demonstrate interaction design — no backend, payment processor, or identity verification service is connected.
